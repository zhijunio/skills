# Requirements analysis

Load for broad scope, complex product review or uncertain requirements. Use only relevant prompts.

## Scope and evidence

Identify the product outcome, primary and secondary actors (including system consumers), stakeholder responsibilities, current process, pain points and evidence. Distinguish user needs from proposed solutions. For an existing product, identify what must remain unchanged.

Maintain separate views of target product capabilities and the currently authorized delivery increment. A capability map groups outcomes by responsibility, not by database table or technology. For each material capability capture purpose, actor or consumer, intended outcome, relevant dependency, scope classification and priority rationale. Avoid inventing a universal module list. If the user requests full product scope, do not demote capabilities to deferred merely because a smaller MVP is possible.

## Priorities and constraints

Record mandatory obligations, desired capabilities and optional or deferred items using existing conventions. Confirm priorities with the decision owner; distinguish dependency ordering from user-value priority. Identify conflicts among desired outcomes, delivery constraints and obligations. Compare alternatives and record accepted tradeoffs, unresolved choices and rationale. Do not assign fabricated scores or deadlines.

Assess relevant quality needs: permissions, privacy, accessibility, performance, availability, integrity, compatibility, recoverability, operability and other applicable obligations. Describe expected user-visible or operational outcomes and conditions, not implementation mechanisms. Record the source of each consequential constraint and any unconfirmed threshold. Detailed quality scenarios and solution design belong with the design owner.

## Review questions

- Who benefits, who operates the product, and who or what can interact with it?
- Which journeys deliver the intended outcomes? What is explicitly excluded?
- Which capabilities are in target scope, current scope, deferred or proposed?
- Are product-level constraints and success signals observable? What evidence is missing?
- Do requirements contradict each other or the existing approved baseline?
- Would a prototype resolve a material user-experience or behavior question more efficiently than further discussion?

If material gaps remain, label the draft and ask only the necessary questions. A review may identify omissions without inventing requirements or editing files.
